\section{Evaluation protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

We fixed the protocol, claims and tests in a document frozen \emph{before} the qualifying run;
this draft reports an exploratory rehearsal of it. The lesson that produced the rules comes first.

\para{A retraction.} An earlier version of our density claim compared numbers from three
different tools: the engine's development harness (four workers per core complex), a Python
harness for Zipformer (one worker per complex), and a third lateness implementation in the bench
suite, each with its own grid and lateness rule. An independent reviewer who read the code, not
our summaries, found that the headline ratio of about \RetractedRatio{}$\times$ compared four workers with one;
per core it was about \RetractedPerCore{}$\times$, and the three lateness rules disagreed. Its seven blocking findings included density quoted without the WER gap and
claims that were not pre-registered. We retracted the claim and rewrote the protocol; a second
pass found items marked fixed that were not implemented, and a third closed most of the rest. The lesson we draw is procedural: \emph{have
an outsider read the code behind each claim before the claim is frozen}.

\para{One harness.} Every density, cost and latency row comes from one tool, which runs
Zipformer (through sherpa-onnx's C API~\cite{sherpaonnx}, version \SherpaVersion, with ONNX
Runtime \OrtVersion), ONNX Runtime~\cite{onnxruntime} and the
\ultra engine through the same scheduler, the same lateness code, the same grid, the same cost
scope and the same placement. Development harnesses are never compared with it.

\para{Equal hardware.} The density unit is one Zen~2 core complex (CCX: four physical cores
sharing 16\,MiB of L3) with four single-threaded workers pinned to its cores; the harness verifies
that the four share one L3 domain and fails closed otherwise. The observer process runs on a
different CCX. Machine-wide results use 15 CCX, with the sixteenth reserved for the observer;
nothing is extrapolated from one CCX to the machine.

\para{Lateness rule A1.} Audio arrives in 20\,ms packets; models consume 160\,ms chunks; the
scheduler ticks on a 320\,ms grid with at most two chunks per step, for every system. At each tick
the lateness of a stream is the age of its oldest undecoded packet relative to its capture
deadline, which never under-reports a chunk's lateness. A configuration passes if, for every
worker, p99 lateness is at most 800\,ms; the mean lateness of the last third of the run exceeds
that of the first third by at most 200\,ms (no growth); and the run drains at cutoff, with
accepted, processed and unfinished audio accounted. CPU time is divided by processed audio only.

\para{Density ladder.} Streams per worker climb 2, 4, \dots, 16, then 20, \dots, 48, three repeats
per rung, alternating systems, 120\,s per rung. The ladder stops at the first rung where any
repeat fails, and the reported density is the last rung before it; a later pass never counts after
an earlier failure. The reported rung must then pass three 300\,s confirmation runs, and a pass at
the top is reported as ``$\ge 48$''. In qualifying mode these settings cannot be overridden. The exploratory
rehearsal reported here used a coarser preset: 4 to 48 streams per worker in steps of 4 (16 per
CCX), 20\,s rungs, one repeat and no confirmation.

\para{Cold boundary and the measured comparison.} For the comparison of \cref{sec:compare} every
recognizer decodes the same staged audio, the 730-utterance DEV panel (\ColdAudioMinutes{}
minutes), one stream at a time, with one inference thread on one pinned physical core, in three
repetitions. Timing starts at recognizer launch and covers every process in its tree through the
flushed final hypothesis and exit: model loading, features, inference, search, output and drain.
Reference preparation, scoring and the observer are outside the boundary. The cost is the median
of user plus system CPU-seconds per audio-second over the three runs, and speed is its
reciprocal; peak resident memory of the tree and the size of the model files are recorded beside
it. One scorer and one text normaliser score every transcript.

\para{One system at a time.} A recognizer that streams its weights through the cache on every
chunk is bound by the memory bus, not by its core: with other benchmark arms running on the same
machine, the same binary on a core that was alone in its cache group cost up to three quarters
more CPU time. A row from the timing host is therefore a primary row only if the machine was
otherwise idle (at most two other busy cores in every repetition), and each receipt records the
busy cores and the clock frequency. Rows from an earlier rotation on a shared host are shown as
interim, in lighter marks, until a quiet row of the same system replaces them. \Cref{tab:hardware} gives
each machine's memory configuration. A system too slow for three full passes is timed on a
73-utterance subset of the panel and takes its WER from one full pass.

\para{Classes, budgets and sessions.} A system is \emph{live streaming} if it emits words while
audio arrives, through its own streaming interface, and \emph{whole-utterance} if it sees the
whole clip first; the two are tabulated and drawn apart. Besides one core, a budget of eight pinned
cores is measured in the mode each system's authors publish: eight threads inside one recognizer,
one process with eight workers that share the weights, or eight separate processes. Rows measured
together form a session. A system is drawn only beside a row of ours from the same session, and
its speed is compared only with rows from the same host; a system measured again in another
session is a cross-check, not a second point. Word lag is measured end to end with the input
paced at real time, at the row's own core budget, against forced alignments of the reference
words~\cite{mcauliffe2017mfa}; the lag the model would have with unbounded compute is recorded
beside it. Energy is read from the processor's energy counters during the same run, by a method
named with each value, and divided by the audio decoded, which gives the watts one live stream
costs. Numbers published by others enter only \cref{tab:reported}.

\para{Warm cost.} Whole-service CPU-seconds per audio-second after model load, from process
rusage, with governor and boost state recorded. Nemotron~\cite{nemotron} runs only as a child
process, so it is excluded from cost and density claims.

\para{Counterbalanced timing.} The cost claim (C-cost) compares each \ultra finalist with Zipformer
in eight blocks. Each block runs every arm once on the same 50 utterances, in the order
Zipformer, dense, multirate in odd blocks and the reverse in even blocks, giving eight paired
$\log$ CPU ratios per comparison. The test is a one-sided paired $t$-test ($df=7$), Holm-corrected
across the two comparisons~\cite{holm1979simple} at $\alpha=\HolmAlpha$; we report the median
ratio with its 95\% bootstrap interval (geometric mean secondary) and both orders' per-block values, so an order effect would be
visible.

\para{Selection and quality.} Checkpoints are selected only on a DEV panel of 730 utterances that
covers every LibriSpeech dev speaker (400 utterances from 40 dev-clean speakers, 330 from 33
dev-other speakers), on single checkpoints and checkpoint averages, ties going to the cheaper
model. Test sets and two sealed out-of-domain sets (VoxPopuli~\cite{wang2021voxpopuli} and AMI
IHM~\cite{carletta2005ami}) are read once each, after the freeze tag, logged in an append-only
ledger whose guards refuse a second read. We disclose that LibriSpeech test-clean and test-other
were read several times before the freeze; no selection used them. WER pools errors and reference words over the utterance population $U$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{WER}(U)=100\,\frac{\sum_{i\in U}(S_i+D_i+I_i)}{\sum_{i\in U}N_i},
\end{equation}
so a pooled panel WER is not the mean of the two split WERs. The quality claim (C-quality) is
the test WER gap to Zipformer with a paired speaker-cluster bootstrap~\cite{bisani2004bootstrap}
interval; a parity claim would require the interval's upper bound within \PreregUpperPP\pp, which we
do not expect.

\para{INT8 gate.} A prepared INT8 artifact is admitted only if, on the panel, its WER exceeds FP32
of the same checkpoint by at most \PreregDeltaPP\pp and the one-sided 95\% bootstrap upper bound of
the difference is at most \PreregUpperPP\pp. The gate tool exits non-zero on failure (an earlier
version exited zero; a reviewer caught it).

\para{Rehearsal catches.} Rehearsing the protocol end to end found defects that document review
could not: placement verification read the main thread's CPU mask, not the worker's, and two routes
to the sealed out-of-domain sets used different ledger keys. Both are fixed with regression tests,
and qualifying mode refuses, fail-closed, a wrong worker count, grid or block order, test audio
before the freeze tag, and an observer sharing L3 with the workers.

\para{Pre-registered claims.} Density is descriptive and always printed beside each system's WER;
cost and quality are tested as above. Shared hosts varied by up to \AASpread\% in a same-day A/A
replay, so every number here is exploratory; the qualified run uses a quiet host of the same CPU
model with other jobs paused.
