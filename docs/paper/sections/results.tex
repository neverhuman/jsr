\subsection{Head-to-Head with Zipformer INT8}\label{sec:versus}

Zipformer INT8 is the strongest system near our cost point and the teacher of our pseudo-labels, so we
compare against it directly on the same utterances and the same core (\cref{tab:versus}). The WER
differences are paired: both systems decode the same 730 utterances, and the bootstrap resamples
speakers. The live stream needs \VsCpuPctOne\% of Zipformer's CPU time (\VsSpeedXOne$\times$ the throughput) and
\VsEnergyPctOne\% of the active core energy per audio second, in \VsRssPctOne\% of the memory, with half
the chunk (\VsChunkOne\,ms vs.\ \VsZipChunk\,ms); its pooled WER is \VsVerdictOne{} Zipformer's (\VsDeltaOne\pp, 95\% bounds \VsLoOne{} to
\VsHiOne\pp). The final text from the same weights is \VsVerdictTwo{} Zipformer's (\VsDeltaTwo\pp, bounds \VsLoTwo{} to \VsHiTwo\pp) at \VsSpeedXTwo$\times$ its
single-core speed. An application that consumes utterance-end text therefore gets Zipformer-class
accuracy from the final pass, and the live stream supplies the always-on, low-latency path at a
fraction of the cost.

\subsection{Paced Always-On Live Capacity on One Quiet Zen~2 Core}\label{sec:capacity}

To measure real-time always-on capacity and stable-word lag under live packet pacing, we run $N\in\{1,4,8,16\}$
concurrent \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} live streams (\texttt{ultra stream}, real-time pacing, 20\,ms packets,
per-packet wakes) pinned to a single quiet Zen~2 core (\cref{tab:capacity}). Sixteen simultaneous live streams occupy
\RelCapSixteenBusy\% of one core at \RelCapSixteenNetW\,W net (\RelCapSixteenGrossW\,W gross) core power per stream
and stable-word lag \RelCapSixteenLagPfifty/\RelCapSixteenLagPninetyFive\,ms (p50/p95). A lone paced stream costs
more per stream (\RelCapOneNetW\,W net, \RelCapOneBusy\% busy; lag \RelCapOneLagPfifty/\RelCapOneLagPninetyFive\,ms)
because it wakes an otherwise idle core at the packet rate and amortises model load over a single 128\,s stream.
Because lag is measured on one continuous 128\,s stream of 22 DEV clips against forced-aligned word ends rather than
per-clip utterances, it is reported separately and not ranked against shared-host per-clip lag measurements.
