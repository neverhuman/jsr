\subsection{Serving density}

\Cref{tab:density,fig:money} are the headline. On one CCX, Zipformer passes the \DenRzeroCCX-stream
rung (\DenRzeroCore{} per core) and fails at \DenFailRzeroCCX; the \ultra multirate model passes
\DenMRCCX{} (\DenMRCore{} per core) and fails at \DenFailMRCCX: \DenRatioMR{}$\times$ the passing load,
with the true ratio bracketed by \DenRatioMRBracketLo{} and \DenRatioMRBracketHi{} by the rung
spacing. The dense finalist passes \DenDenseCCX. At the shared 32-stream rung the multirate model
costs \CostLadderMR{} \cpus against \CostLadderRzero{} (\CostRatioMR{}$\times$ less), and a fixed
equal-load probe at \ProbeStreams{} streams for \ProbeSeconds\,s gave worst-worker p99 of
\ProbePninetyNineMR{} (multirate), \ProbePninetyNineDense{} (dense) and \ProbePninetyNineRzero\,ms
(Zipformer, over the bound), all drained.

\para{Why density exceeds cost.} The density ratio (\DenRatioMR{}$\times$) is larger than the cost
ratio (\CostRatioMR{}$\times$) for two measured reasons. At its passing rung Zipformer keeps the
workers only \UtilClaimRzero\% busy, against \UtilClaimMR\% for the multirate model: Zipformer fails
on lateness before it runs out of CPU. And rule A1 counts the time a packet waits for its chunk to
fill, which penalises Zipformer's 320\,ms chunk against our 160\,ms chunk. Batching does not explain
the gain: batching sixteen streams lowers our cost per stream by at most \BatchLift\%; the gain is
per-stream work.

\para{Single-stream cost.} In the eight counterbalanced blocks the pre-registered median ratio of
CPU time to Zipformer's was \AbabMedianMR{} for the multirate engine (95\% bootstrap CI
\AbabMedianMRLo--\AbabMedianMRHi, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmMR) and \AbabMedianDense{} for the
dense step-45k engine (\AbabMedianDenseLo--\AbabMedianDenseHi, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmDense);
the geometric means are \AbabRatioMR{} and \AbabRatioDense.

\para{What these numbers are, and are not.} Zipformer runs through sherpa-onnx's C API with one
thread per worker and its batched multi-stream decode, at its released 320\,ms chunk; we did not
sweep its configuration. The ladder used 20\,s rungs, so ``p99'' is close to the maximum lateness
of the run. Each stream cycles the first \LadderUtts{} dev-clean utterances rather than the panel.
The rehearsal used rungs of 4 to 48 streams per worker in steps of 4, one of the 16 CCX, and
nothing is extrapolated to the machine. The multirate model's fleet memory is higher than
Zipformer's (\DenRssMR{} vs.\ \DenRssRzero\,GB).

\subsection{Bit-exactness across ISAs and INT8 gates}


With the platform \texttt{libm}, FP32 decodes already agreed across ISAs, but INT8 decodes did
not: 1-ULP feature differences moved per-row quantisation ties, so between \XisaRfourPre{} and
\XisaPreDtwoFortyFive{} of 730 utterances matched. With the portable front end, all
\XisaAgree{} of \XisaTotal{} decodes are identical (\cref{tab:xisa}), and every INT8 artifact
passes its gate with the worst upper bound at \GateMaxUpper\pp, well inside the pre-registered
\PreregUpperPP\pp. Switching the front end changed no FP32 transcript.

\subsection{Ablations}


\Cref{tab:ablate} sizes the cheap levers. Search error is large: beam 4 removes
\BeamGainClean\pp / \BeamGainOther\pp on full dev-clean / dev-other at step \DtwoStep{}
(\cref{tab:acc}), with a similar gain at step 50k (\cref{tab:ablate}), and the engine's native
beam takes the multirate model from \LeftOneTwentyEightWer\% to \MRBeamPanelBeamInt\% pooled on the
panel, both INT8, while passing the INT8 gate. Untrained 640\,ms
chunks gain \ChunkSixteenGainOther\pp on dev-other, but untrained full attention hurts dev-clean. Halving the left context
of the multirate model to 64 frames costs only \LeftSixtyFourDelta\pp, cuts attention time to about
\LeftSixtyFourAttn{} of its original and halves the key/value state; it is a candidate density knob
that has not yet been run through the ladder. Thirty-two frames costs \LeftThirtyTwoDelta\pp
without retraining.
