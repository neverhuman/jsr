The cold comparison of \cref{sec:protocol} (same staged DEV audio, one stream, one pinned core,
model load included, Zipformer, Moonshine, Whisper, Nemotron and \ultra) is running at the time

\subsection{Warm cost and stream density}

\emph{Scope: warm, in-process, single harness, exploratory.} \Cref{tab:density,fig:density}
report the single-harness rehearsal: Zipformer, the dense model (step 45k and the averaged
finalist) and the multirate model at step 50k on the same host, grid, lateness rule, workers and placement, each with the WER
of the checkpoint that was timed. Later, more accurate checkpoints (\cref{tab:acc}) are not
paired with these timings.

\para{Density.} On one CCX with four workers, Zipformer passes \DenRzeroCCX{} streams
(p99 \DenPninetyNineRzero\,ms) and fails the next rung; the \ultra dense finalist passes
\DenDenseCCX{} in one run (the step-45k model reached \DenDenseFortyFiveRangeLo--\DenDenseFortyFiveRangeHi{}
across runs A and D; p99 \DenPninetyNineDense\,ms) and the multirate model \DenMRCCX{} (p99
\DenPninetyNineMR\,ms): \DenRatioDense{}$\times$ and \DenRatioMR{}$\times$ Zipformer's density, or
\DenMRCore{} live streams per physical core for the multirate model. The multirate model sustains
\DenRatioMRvsDenseLo--\DenRatioMRvsDenseHi{}$\times$ the dense density at equal width (exploratory;
the two come from different training runs, so this is not an architecture-only ablation).

\para{Single-stream cost.} In the eight counterbalanced blocks, the pre-registered median ratio
of CPU time to Zipformer's was \AbabMedianDense{} for the dense engine (95\% bootstrap CI
\AbabMedianDenseLo--\AbabMedianDenseHi, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmDense) and \AbabMedianMR{} for
the multirate engine (\AbabMedianMRLo--\AbabMedianMRHi, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmMR); the
geometric means are \AbabRatioDense{} and \AbabRatioMR. The advantage widens with
load: at sixteen streams per worker the ratios are \FrontierRatioDense{}$\times$ and
\FrontierRatioMR{}$\times$, because Zipformer's single-stream overheads amortise less than our
fused engine's.

\subsection{Bit-exactness across ISAs and INT8 gates}


With the platform \texttt{libm}, FP32 decodes already agreed across ISAs, but INT8 decodes did
not: 1-ULP feature differences moved per-row quantisation ties, so between \XisaRfourPre{} and
\XisaPreDtwoFortyFive{} of 730 utterances matched. With the portable front end, all
\XisaAgree{} of \XisaTotal{} decodes are identical (\cref{tab:xisa}), and every INT8 artifact
passes its gate with the worst upper bound at \GateMaxUpper\pp, well inside the pre-registered
\PreregUpperPP\pp. Switching the front end changed no FP32 transcript.

\subsection{Ablations}


\Cref{tab:ablate} sizes the cheap levers. Search error is large: beam 4 removes
\BeamGainClean\pp / \BeamGainOther\pp on full dev-clean / dev-other at step \DtwoStep{}
(\cref{tab:acc}), with a similar gain at step 50k (\cref{tab:ablate}), and the engine's native
beam takes the multirate model from \LeftOneTwentyEightWer\% to \MRBeamPanelBeamInt\% pooled on the
panel, both INT8, while passing the INT8 gate. Untrained 640\,ms
chunks gain \ChunkSixteenGainOther\pp on dev-other, but untrained full attention hurts dev-clean. Halving the left context
of the multirate model to 64 frames costs only \LeftSixtyFourDelta\pp, cuts attention time to about
\LeftSixtyFourAttn{} of its original and halves the key/value state; it is a candidate density knob
that has not yet been run through the ladder. Thirty-two frames costs \LeftThirtyTwoDelta\pp
without retraining.
