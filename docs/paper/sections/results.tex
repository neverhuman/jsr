\subsection{Serving density}\label{sec:density}

The cold comparison above times one stream at a time. A service runs many, so we also measure
paced density in the warm harness of \cref{sec:protocol}, for \ultra and Zipformer only and for
earlier checkpoints than the one in \cref{fig:money}; each row carries its own checkpoint's WER
(\cref{tab:density,fig:frontier}). On one CCX, Zipformer passes the \DenRzeroCCX-stream
rung (\DenRzeroCore{} per core) and fails at \DenFailRzeroCCX; the \ultra multirate model passes
\DenMRCCX{} (\DenMRCore{} per core) and fails at \DenFailMRCCX: \DenRatioMR{}$\times$ the passing load,
with the true ratio bracketed by \DenRatioMRBracketLo{} and \DenRatioMRBracketHi{} by the rung
spacing. The latest dense checkpoint (average of steps 55/60/65k) passes \DenDenseCCX{} with greedy
decoding \emph{and} with beam 4: beam search, which lowers its pooled WER from
\WerRehearsalDensePooled\% to \WerRehearsalDenseBeamPooled\%, costs no density. At the shared 32-stream rung the multirate model
costs \CostLadderMR{} \cpus against \CostLadderRzero{} (\CostRatioMR{}$\times$ less), and a fixed
equal-load probe at \ProbeStreams{} streams for \ProbeSeconds\,s gave worst-worker p99 of
\ProbePninetyNineMR{} (multirate), \ProbePninetyNineDenseOld{} (dense, run E) and \ProbePninetyNineRzero\,ms
(Zipformer, over the bound), all drained.

\para{Why density exceeds cost.} The density ratio (\DenRatioMR{}$\times$) is larger than the cost
ratio (\CostRatioMR{}$\times$). At the first failing load, the two \ultra
configurations approached the four cores' projected capacity: \DemandFailDenseOld\% (dense, run E) and
\DemandFailMR\% (multirate) at the first failed rung, against \DemandDenseOld\% and \DemandMR\% at the
passing rung. It is compute-bound and uses nearly all of the hardware. Zipformer passes at
\DemandRzero\% and fails at \DemandFailRzero\%, with a fifth of the CPU still unused. That is
consistent with a scheduling limit under the tested chunk and pacing geometry (rule A1 also counts
time a packet waits for its chunk to fill, and Zipformer's chunk is 320\,ms against our 160\,ms),
but the receipts do not isolate chunk size. Batching does not explain our gain (it saves at most
\BatchLift\% per stream). Every passing rung processed all accepted audio
(\AudioProcessedMR\,s for multirate), none unfinished.

\para{Single-stream cost.} In eight counterbalanced blocks the pre-registered median CPU ratio to
Zipformer was \AbabMedianMR{} (multirate, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmMR) and \AbabMedianDense{}
(dense step 45k, $p_\text{Holm}=$\,\AbabPholmDense); intervals are in \cref{tab:density}.

\para{What these numbers are, and are not.} Zipformer runs through sherpa-onnx's C API with one
thread per worker and its batched multi-stream decode, at its released 320\,ms chunk; we did not
sweep its configuration. The ladder used 20\,s rungs, so ``p99'' is close to the maximum lateness
of the run. Each stream cycles the first \LadderUtts{} dev-clean utterances; one of the 16 CCX was used,
and nothing is extrapolated to the machine. The multirate model's fleet memory is higher than
Zipformer's (\DenRssMR{} vs.\ \DenRssRzero\,GB).

\subsection{Bit-exactness across ISAs and INT8 gates}


With the platform \texttt{libm}, FP32 decodes already agreed across ISAs, but INT8 decodes did
not: 1-ULP feature differences moved per-row quantisation ties, so between \XisaRfourPre{} and
\XisaPreDtwoFortyFive{} of 730 utterances matched. With the portable front end, all
\XisaAgree{} of \XisaTotal{} decodes are identical (\cref{tab:xisa}), and every INT8 artifact
passes its gate with the worst upper bound at \GateMaxUpper\pp, well inside the pre-registered
\PreregUpperPP\pp. Switching the front end changed no FP32 transcript.

\subsection{Ablations}


\Cref{tab:ablate} sizes the cheap levers. Search error is large: beam 4 removes
\BeamGainClean\pp / \BeamGainOther\pp on full dev-clean / dev-other at step \DtwoStep{}
(\cref{tab:acc}), with a similar gain at step 50k (\cref{tab:ablate}), and the engine's native
beam takes the multirate model from \LeftOneTwentyEightWer\% to \MRBeamPanelBeamInt\% pooled on the
panel, both INT8, while passing the INT8 gate. Untrained 640\,ms
chunks gain \ChunkSixteenGainOther\pp on dev-other, but untrained full attention hurts dev-clean. Halving the multirate model's left context
costs only \LeftSixtyFourDelta\pp and halves key/value state, a density knob not yet laddered.
