\subsection{Head-to-Head with Zipformer INT8}\label{sec:versus}

Zipformer INT8 is the strongest system near our cost point and the teacher of our pseudo-labels, so we
compare against it directly on the same utterances and the same core (\cref{tab:versus}), with Zipformer timed
in the same interleaved blocks as \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{}; speed, energy and memory ratios are medians of the
within-block ratios. The WER
differences are paired: both systems decode the same 730 utterances, and the bootstrap resamples
speakers. These 730 utterances overlap the half of DEV used to select the \ReleaseVer{} checkpoint and decoding
policy, so the gaps below are selection-panel gaps; on the unselected half of DEV (C, \cref{sec:candidate}) the live
stream with the language model trails Zipformer by \CandLiveLmCDelta\pp (bounds \CandLiveLmCLo{} to \CandLiveLmCHi). The live stream needs \VsCpuPctOne\% of Zipformer's CPU time (\VsSpeedXOne$\times$ the throughput) and
\VsEnergyPctOne\% of the active core energy per audio second, in \VsRssPctOne\% of the memory, with half
the chunk (\VsChunkOne\,ms vs.\ \VsZipChunk\,ms); its pooled WER is \VsVerdictOne{} Zipformer's (\VsDeltaOne\pp, one-sided 95\% bounds \VsLoOne{} to
\VsHiOne\pp). The final text from the same weights is \VsVerdictTwo{} Zipformer's (\VsDeltaTwo\pp, bounds \VsLoTwo{} to \VsHiTwo\pp) at \VsSpeedXTwo$\times$ its
single-core speed and \VsEnergyPctTwo\% of its active core energy per audio second. For an application that consumes utterance-end text, the final pass therefore matches
Zipformer's accuracy within bounds that do not resolve a difference (inconclusive on both the selection panel and
the unselected half), and the live stream supplies the always-on, low-latency path at a fraction of the cost.

\subsection{The Registered Look at \ReleaseVer{} (RC2)}\label{sec:candidate}
The \ReleaseVer{} acoustic model (RC2), with the same shape and per-frame cost as \PriorVer{}, was trained on \CandTrainKh\,h of
audio, adding Libri-Light large to the release lineage, and was frozen on the selection half of DEV
before one registered look at the panel and the unselected half (C, \CandCUtts{} utterances).
\Cref{tab:candidate} reports every registered test, including the ones that failed. On the panel,
the 160\,ms live stream with the compact live LM lowers WER against the shipped release by
\CandLiveLmOldDelta\pp (bounds \CandLiveLmOldLo{} to \CandLiveLmOldHi), but its gap to Zipformer INT8 is
\CandLiveLmPanelDelta\pp with an upper bound of \CandLiveLmPanelHi\pp, so non-inferiority at the registered
\CandNiMargin\pp margin is not shown (Holm-adjusted $p=\CandHolmNi$). The panel overlaps the selection half;
on C the same live gap is \CandLiveLmCDelta\pp (bounds \CandLiveLmCLo{} to \CandLiveLmCHi). The final pass
reaches \CandFinalWer\% on the panel; its differences from Zipformer (\CandFinalPanelDelta\pp panel,
\CandFinalCDelta\pp C) and from the shipped final pass (\CandFinalOldDelta\pp) are not significant
(Holm-adjusted $p=\CandHolmFinal$ and $\CandHolmOld$). RC2 made \CandFinalFullEdits{} full-DEV final edits
against a registered adoption gate of \CandGateFinalFull{} and missed both panel targets
(\CandGateLivePanel{} live, \CandGateFinalPanel{} final edits). \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} is released by owner
decision with these misses stated; it is not a gate pass. Live decoding with the language model also trails
Zipformer INT8 on the unselected half of DEV (\CandLiveLmCDelta\pp).
At freeze, the \PriorVer{} baseline was mispinned to a historical checkpoint; the comparisons above pair the
same RC2 transcripts with the shipped \PriorVer{} transcripts, with no new decode. \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} keeps the
engine's exactness: \CandIsaRuns{} witness decodes on an Intel Xeon (AVX2 and AVX-512 VNNI dispatch) and an
Apple M3 (NEON), covering live greedy at one and sixteen streams, both live LM policies and the final pass,
reproduce the confirmatory look's Zen~2 transcripts on every panel utterance, with identical token streams
within each live policy. Those witnesses cover the three SIMD backends of the \ReleaseVer{} binary, which was built at
\ScalarRelCommit{}. On main from \ScalarMainCommit{}, which adds a bit-exact scalar path, transcripts are also
identical on the forced-scalar backend: AVX2, AVX-512 VNNI, NEON and forced scalar give \ScalarDiffs{}/\ScalarUtts{}
differing utterances for greedy and both live LM policies, on x86 and an Apple M3. That four-backend claim does not
apply to the \ReleaseVer{} binary.

\subsection{Paced Always-On Live Capacity on One Quiet Zen~2 Core}\label{sec:capacity}

To measure real-time always-on capacity and stable-word lag under live packet pacing, we run $N\in\{1,4,8,16\}$
concurrent \ultra{} \PriorVer{} live streams (\texttt{ultra stream}, real-time pacing, 20\,ms packets,
per-packet wakes) pinned to a single quiet Zen~2 core (\cref{tab:capacity}); this capacity run was not repeated
for \ReleaseVer{}. Sixteen simultaneous live streams occupy
\RelCapSixteenBusy\% of one core at \RelCapSixteenNetW\,W net (\RelCapSixteenGrossW\,W gross) core power per stream
and stable-word lag \RelCapSixteenLagPfifty/\RelCapSixteenLagPninetyFive\,ms (p50/p95). A lone paced stream costs
more per stream (\RelCapOneNetW\,W net, \RelCapOneBusy\% busy; lag \RelCapOneLagPfifty/\RelCapOneLagPninetyFive\,ms)
because it wakes an otherwise idle core at the packet rate and amortises model load over a single 128\,s stream.
Because lag is measured on one continuous 128\,s stream of 22 DEV clips against forced-aligned word ends rather than
per-clip utterances, it is reported separately and not ranked against shared-host per-clip lag measurements.
