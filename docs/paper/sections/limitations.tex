\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\para{Release gates and cost budget.} The v4.2.0 final pass meets its registered edit gates (panel \FinPanelEdits{} vs.\
\CandGateFinalPanel, full DEV \FinFullEdits{} vs.\ \CandGateFinalFull). v4.2.1 live meets its registered panel gate (\LivPanelEdits{} edits vs.\ \CandGateLivePanel), which v4.1.0/v4.2.0
live missed (\LookLivePanelEdits) and shipped by owner decision; its endpoint rerank setting (margin \LivSkipMargin) was chosen by
the owner from timed research settings. The final pass's confidence gate
was locked by owner choice when research timing put it just over its +\FinGateBudgetPct\% CPU budget; in R9 it sits at that
budget (\FinGateCpuPct\%, median of within-block ratios), so on this host it neither clearly meets nor exceeds it. Both passes were selected on
the DEV selection half and checked once on the unselected half; the gate margin and reranker were fitted on training audio.
The live rerank adds \LivCostRssMb\,MB of peak memory and \LivDelayPNinetyFive\,ms p95 of compute at each utterance end on one
core; it was not timed in a paced microphone stream, and \texttt{isosyll stream} does not use it. Its R10 cost
against v4.2.0 live (\LivCostCpuPct\% CPU, \LivCostEnergyPct\% energy, +\LivCostRssMb\,MB, +\LivDelayPNinetyFive\,ms endpoint p95) is well above
the research estimate from paced ARM timing (about \LivResCpuPct\% process CPU and +\LivResEndpointPNinetyFive\,ms endpoint p95) that the
release decision relied on. The added memory matches the size of the final pass's 5-gram pack (\LmPackMb\,MB), which the live process now loads
for the rerank alongside its own LM.

\para{Utterance-segmented final mode and memory.} \ultra{} live streams continuously without an endpoint
detector. \ultra{} final needs utterance endpoints (capped at 40\,s) and raises peak RSS to \ColdHeadRss\,MB because of the
5-gram LM pack (\LmPackMb\,MB on disk). The v4.2.0 final pass runs in file mode only: streaming keeps the v4.1.0
commit pass (median post-endpoint delay \RelCommitDelayPfifty\,ms, from an earlier checkpoint of identical cost),
because live and commit share one encoder state there; the new pass was not delay-tested, and loading a second
artifact for streaming finals remains future work, as does shrinking memory without losing accuracy.

\para{Live accuracy and next lever.} The live stream trades accuracy for extreme speed and
low power: on the unselected DEV C half its WER is \LivCZipDelta\pp from Zipformer INT8's (bounds \LivCZipLo{} to
\LivCZipHi) only since the v4.2.1 utterance-end rerank (\cref{sec:versus}); its compact live LM and the rerank's LM and
rescorer raise peak memory to \ColdLiveRss\,MB. Most live repair positions (\VfDiagInteriorPct\%) are common interior words on hard
audio, and probes of reranking, regularisation, and relabelling existing audio did not move clean live
WER; further gains at this compute budget will need fresh acoustic diversity and stronger
non-Zipformer supervision.

\para{Training data and LM overlap.} \ultra{} is trained on LibriSpeech 960\,h plus Libri-Light
pseudo-labelled by R0 (the Zipformer comparator). Release comparisons use read English LibriSpeech DEV
audio, which is in-domain for both \ultra{} and Zipformer; rankings on conversational, accented, or
noisy speech may differ. The OpenSLR-11 training text of \texttt{M5.ulm} contains \LmOverlapCount{} of
the \LmOverlapOf{} longer DEV transcripts verbatim; removing them leaves the relative LM WER gain
unchanged.

\para{Host coverage, energy scope, and DEV-only claims.} \ColdHostSentence{} \ColdSubsetSentence{}
Energy is active core (CPU-domain) joules per audio-second from RAPL registers, never board or wall
power, measured unpaced; every plotted comparator is idle-subtracted in the R7 campaign on a
\RsevenClips-clip subset while CI jobs ran on other cores, and \ultra{} in the R9 (final) and R10 (live) full-panel blocks under the same protocol,
which also give the paired ratio. All accuracy numbers are LibriSpeech DEV only;
as disclosed in \cref{sec:protocol}, test-clean and test-other were read several times before our
benchmark freeze.
