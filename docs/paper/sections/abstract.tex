\begin{abstract}
Streaming speech services must bound decode lateness as concurrent audio arrives.
We measure a streaming transducer with multirate encoding and prepared INT8
inference. In \DenRungSeconds-second replay rungs on one four-core Zen~2 complex,
D2MR at step 50,000 passed at \DenMRCCX{} streams versus \DenRzeroCCX{} for
Zipformer INT8, a \DenRatioMR-fold difference in passing load. Worst-worker p99
lateness was \DenPninetyNineMR{} and \DenPninetyNineRzero\,ms, respectively,
under an 800-ms deadline. Both passing configurations drained all accepted
audio. The dense checkpoint passed at \DenDenseCCX{} streams in the same
replay. At the common \MatchedRung-stream load, D2MR processed
\AudioPerCpuMR{} audio seconds per CPU second versus \AudioPerCpuRzero{} for
Zipformer, corresponding to \CostLadderMR{} and \CostLadderRzero{} CPU seconds
per audio second. At \ProbeStreams{} streams in a \ProbeSeconds-second probe,
worst-worker p99 was \ProbePninetyNineMR{} versus \ProbePninetyNineRzero\,ms.
On the 400-utterance dev-clean selection panel, the same configurations yield
\WerRehearsalMR\% and \WerRehearsalRzero\% word error rate. These shared-host
results are exploratory. Matched cold costs, hour-long capacity confirmation,
and registered test accuracy remain pending.
\end{abstract}
