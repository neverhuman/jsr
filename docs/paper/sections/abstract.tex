\begin{abstract}
An always-on speech recogniser pays for every second of audio, speech or not, so its compute, memory
and energy per stream matter as much as its word error rate (WER). \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} pairs a
compact streaming Conformer transducer with a pure-Rust engine built on per-row INT8 quantisation,
exact integer accumulation and a portable log-mel front end; one prepared INT8 artifact serves two
operating points. \emph{Cost:} on the same 730 LibriSpeech development utterances and the same pinned Zen~2 core,
timed in the same interleaved blocks, the 160\,ms live stream with a compact language model delivers
\VsSpeedXOne$\times$ the single-core throughput of streaming Zipformer INT8 at \VsEnergyPctOne\% of its
active core energy and \VsRssPctOne\% of its memory, with half its chunk length.
\emph{Accuracy:} on this selection panel its pooled WER is \VsVerdictOne{} Zipformer's (\VsDeltaOne{} percentage points,
paired one-sided 95\% bounds \VsLoOne{} to \VsHiOne{}, which include zero), but on the unselected half of DEV the gap is
\CandLiveLmCDelta{} points (bounds \CandLiveLmCLo{} to \CandLiveLmCHi). At utterance
ends, a commit pass on the same weights reaches \ColdHeadWer\% pooled WER, \VsVerdictTwo{} Zipformer's
\ColdZipWer\% (paired bounds \VsLoTwo{} to \VsHiTwo), at \VsSpeedXTwo$\times$ its speed and \VsEnergyPctTwo\% of its active core energy.
\ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} is released by owner decision, not as a gate pass: its registered full-DEV final gate was
missed (\CandFinalFullEdits{} edits against \CandGateFinalFull). \CandIsaRuns{} witness decodes on other x86
and ARM CPUs reproduce its Zen~2 transcripts exactly.
\emph{What did not help:} reranking the beam, stronger teacher labels, lookahead convolution and regularisation
did not move live accuracy; the residual sits in common interior words on hard audio, so fresh audio is the next lever.
All results are exploratory and use development data only.
\end{abstract}
