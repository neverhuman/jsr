\begin{abstract}
On one four-core Zen~2 core complex, the \ultra multirate streaming transducer sustains
\DenMRCore{} live streams per physical core (\DenMRCCX{} per complex) with worst-worker p99
lateness under 800\,ms, against \DenRzeroCore{} per core for a pretrained Zipformer INT8 in the
same harness: \DenRatioMR{}$\times$ the passing load (bracket
\DenRatioMRBracketLo--\DenRatioMRBracketHi{} given the rung spacing), identical in all
\DenReproRuns{} rehearsal runs, with all accepted audio drained. At an equal load of
\MatchedRung{} streams it processes \AudioPerCpuMR{} audio-seconds per CPU-second against
\AudioPerCpuRzero{} (\CostRatioMR{}$\times$ less CPU), and at \ProbeStreams{} streams its worst-worker
p99 is \ProbePninetyNineMR{} against \ProbePninetyNineRzero\,ms. Accuracy lags, and training
continues: \WerRehearsalMRPooled\% against \WerRzeroPanel\% pooled WER on the same 730-utterance
DEV panel. A dense model sustains \DenDenseCore{} streams per core at \WerRehearsalDensePooled\%.
The engine behind this is pure Rust and integer-exact: per-row centred INT8 makes each stream's
output independent of its batch, within \GateMaxDelta\pp WER of FP32, and with a portable log-mel
front end the tokens decoded from raw PCM are bit-identical on x86 AVX2 and ARM NEON
(\XisaAgree/\XisaTotal{} decodes). All results are exploratory (\DenRungSeconds\,s ladder rungs,
shared host); cold costs, hour-long confirmation and registered test accuracy are pending.
\end{abstract}
