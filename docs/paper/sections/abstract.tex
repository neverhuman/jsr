\begin{abstract}
We present \ultra, a streaming Conformer transducer with a pure-Rust integer engine for CPU speech
recognition, and state its accuracy first. Trained on LibriSpeech 960\,h only, our best model
reaches \WerDtwoBestClean\% / \WerDtwoBestOther\% WER on full dev-clean / dev-other (2{,}703 /
2{,}864 utterances); a pretrained Zipformer reaches \WerRzeroDevClean\% on the first 400 dev-clean
utterances. \ultra is markedly less accurate. What it buys is cost: in a warm, in-process
rehearsal of our pre-registered single-harness protocol (p99 lateness $\le$ 800\,ms, four workers
on one Zen~2 core complex), the multirate model at step 50k sustains \DenMRCCX{} live streams and
the dense model at step 45k \DenDenseCCX, against \DenRzeroCCX{} for Zipformer
(\DenRatioMR{}$\times$ and \DenRatioDense{}$\times$), at \WerRehearsalMR\% and
\WerRehearsalDense\% WER against Zipformer's \WerRehearsalRzero\% on the same 400 dev-clean panel
utterances, scored in the same harness. The engine is integer-exact: per-row
centred INT8 makes each stream's output independent of its batch, and with a portable log-mel
front end the tokens decoded from raw PCM are bit-identical on x86 AVX2 and ARM NEON
(\XisaAgree/\XisaTotal{} decodes), with INT8 within \GateMaxDelta\pp WER of FP32. All numbers are
exploratory; an earlier cross-harness density claim was retracted after independent review.
\end{abstract}
