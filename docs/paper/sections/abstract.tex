\begin{abstract}
An always-on speech recogniser pays for every second of audio, speech or not, so its compute, memory
and energy per stream matter as much as its word error rate (WER). \ultra{} \ReleaseVer{} pairs a
compact streaming Conformer transducer with a pure-Rust engine built on per-row INT8 quantisation,
exact integer accumulation and a portable log-mel front end. One prepared INT8 artifact serves two
operating points. On the same 730 LibriSpeech development utterances and the same pinned Zen~2 core,
the 160\,ms live stream delivers \VsSpeedXOne$\times$ the single-core throughput of streaming
Zipformer INT8 at \VsEnergyPctOne\% of its active core energy and \VsRssPctOne\% of its memory, with
half its chunk length; its pooled WER is \VsVerdictOne{} Zipformer's by \VsDeltaOne{} percentage
points (paired one-sided 95\% bounds \VsLoOne{} to \VsHiOne). At utterance ends, a commit pass on the same
weights reaches \ColdHeadWer\% pooled WER, \VsVerdictTwo{} Zipformer's \ColdZipWer\% (paired bounds
\VsLoTwo{} to \VsHiTwo), at \VsSpeedXTwo$\times$ its speed. Sixteen paced live streams share one core at
\RelCapSixteenBusy\% load with a 95th-percentile stable-word lag of \RelCapSixteenLagPninetyFive\,ms.
\RelIsaProof{} Greedy INT8 changes full-DEV pooled WER by \RelIntEightDelta{} percentage points relative to FP32 PyTorch
(one-sided 95\% bounds \RelIntEightLo{} to \RelIntEightHi). Over \TrainTotalKh\,h of training audio, the model
saturates on LibriSpeech 960\,h alone, and Zipformer-pseudo-labelled Libri-Light then lowers live WER
by \TrainLiveGain{} points. All results are exploratory and use development data only.
\end{abstract}
